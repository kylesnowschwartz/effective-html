How each week's restock order is built

Acme's warehouse turns twelve weeks of recorded demand into a purchase plan by adding four rules on top of it. Scroll to add one rule at a time; the chart rebuilds at every step. The sliders let you change the two assumptions that are judgement calls, not facts.

Step 1 · the record

Start with what actually shipped

Twelve weeks of units shipped from the Riverside warehouse. Nothing is adjusted yet — this is the raw record the whole plan is built on.

Step 2 · the baseline

Find the ordinary week

A single week is noisy, so the plan works from the twelve-week average. Weeks above the line are the ones that drain stock faster than resupply replaces it.

Step 3 · the buffer

Add cover for a bad week

Demand runs ahead of forecast about as often as it runs behind, so every week's order carries a safety buffer on top of recorded demand. The buffer percentage is a policy choice — move the slider and watch the orange band grow.

Step 4 · the supplier floor

Round up to the supplier's minimum

The supplier will not ship a pallet below its minimum order. Quiet weeks get topped up to that floor whether the warehouse needs the units or not, which is where most of the excess stock comes from.

Step 5 · the ceiling

Trim anything the shelves cannot hold

Riverside holds 320 units of this line. Any planned order above that is cut back before it is sent — the supplier top-up goes first, then the buffer. Turn the cap off to see the order the rules would produce with unlimited space.
